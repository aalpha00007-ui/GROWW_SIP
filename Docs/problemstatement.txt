## 1. Context

### 1.1 The problem
Salaried 25–34-year-olds who start their first SIP with good intent but no named goal lose their reason to continue the first time a market fall or a news item sends them into the app and the number looks wrong. Nothing at that moment tells them what the SIP is for or what is normal at month 3 or month 9, so short-term returns become the yardstick. The exit is then clearer than the alternatives: they learn how to stop from YouTube, misread what cancelling does, and miss skip or a lower amount.

This is not "investors stop when markets fall". A fall prompts the check; it does not decide the outcome. In our survey, the Groww users who check after market moves and have a goal never considered stopping. The one who wavered had no goal and judged by "few Mutual funds didn't gave profit".

### 1.2 Why build it
| Evidence | Source |
| --- | --- |
| 21 Groww tutorials in one year explain how to stop, skip or cancel a SIP; 46 user comments sit under them | Discovery engine, Groww-only set (155 posts) |
| 7 users thought cancelling refunds money or removes units; 5 did not know about pause or how to restart; 11 could not delete autopay | Discovery engine, coded by hand |
| Only 1 Groww post blames a market fall | Discovery engine |
| 0 of 2,181 Groww posts name a goal such as retirement or a home | Discovery engine, raw data |
| 3 of 4 Groww survey users check weekly or daily after market news; the only waverer had no specific goal | Primary survey, 23 Oct 2026 |
| Direct mutual funds are Groww's customer acquisition, engagement and retention engine; stocks and derivatives were 62% of income in fiscal Q2 FY27 | [Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/inside-growws-revenue-mix-what-comes-after-broking/), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/groww-q2-profit-jumps-54-yoy-to-₹725-cr-revenue-rises-47/) |
| Industry: 49.68 lakh SIPs closed vs 63.26 lakh opened in September 2026 (stoppage ratio 78.53%) | [5paisa](https://www.5paisa.com/blog/sip-stoppage-ratio-falls-78-53-percent-record-inflows) |

### 1.3 Target user and moments
- **User:** a salaried 25–34-year-old in their first 12 months of SIP investing, who started to "grow my money" or "build a habit" without naming a goal. Users with irregular monthly surplus are most exposed.
- **Moment 1, set:** the SIP box, just before Start SIP. Intent is highest here, and the goal costs one tap.
- **Moment 2, see:** a market fall or news sends them into the app; the SIP is red or flat. The goal must already be on screen.
- **Moment 3, recall:** they doubt the SIP and head to Stop. The goal they set comes back, with their own facts and the alternatives.

### 1.4 Goals and non-goals
**Goals**
1. Give every first-year investor an anchor: their own goal, set at the start and shown at the moments of doubt.
2. Turn "stop" into an informed choice between keep, skip, pause, lower amount and stop.
3. Prove an eval-gated AI feature: measured against the Evidence Log golden set, with a visible quality check and a safe fallback.
4. Put the feature inside a familiar app: every product Groww offers on its app and website is present, kept simple, so testers do the task in a setting they recognise.

**Non-goals**
- Recommending funds, predicting returns, or telling anyone to continue or stop.
- Making stopping harder. Stop anyway stays one tap.
- Real money, KYC, real market data or real orders in any product.
- Copying Groww's logo or colours, or presenting the prototype as Groww's own app (see 1.5).
- Fixing autopay reliability (the adjacent cause; Phase 2).

### 1.5 Scope of the prototype
**What the AI may use (only this, nothing beyond it)**
| Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- |
| This investor's own facts in the prototype database (SIP amount, start date, instalments paid, skipped and planned, invested, current value, worst fall since start, goal and target month) | Any real person's data, real funds or real NAVs |
| Fixed explainer text we write for four mechanics: what stop does, what skip does, what pause does, what lowering the amount does | Web search, third-party blogs, news, forums |
| Two official links: [SEBI Investor Website](https://investor.sebi.gov.in/index.html) and [AMFI](https://www.amfiindia.com) | Fund comparisons, return figures beyond the investor's own, any "best fund" content |

**What the app contains:** Groww's product set and screen structure (bottom tabs, product tabs on Explore, fund page with the SIP box, SIP page controls, orders, portfolio), each product at one of three depths: Full, Simple or Placeholder (Appendix E). Only the SIP journey and the goal are built in full.

**Brand:** the app is called **GROWW_SIP**. It follows Groww's structure but uses its own logo and colours, and every page says "Case-study prototype · Not affiliated with Groww". A public money app that looks identical to the real one could be mistaken for it, and mentors can judge the feature without that. All funds, stocks and prices are made up (for example "Banyan Flexi Cap Fund, simulated").

### 2.3 Recommendation
All three apps offer the mechanics (skip, pause, change), but on the SIP page, before the investor decides, and none asks what the money is for. At the stop moment they ask for a reason from a fixed list and offer stop or go back. GROWW_SIP keeps the existing mechanics and adds two things: a goal asked once in the SIP box and carried to Home, the SIP page and the stop flow; and a stop flow that reads the reason, shows the investor's own facts and goal, and puts the alternatives inside it.

### 3.2 User journey (the mentor task follows it)
1. **Start.** Welcome, tick the box, start as Riya (F1).
2. **Set the goal.** Explore › Mutual funds › Banyan Flexi Cap Fund › SIP box: Rs 2,000, monthly on 2nd, pick Home and 2031 › Start SIP (F2). If they chose "Not sure yet", the success screen asks once more (F3).
3. **See it.** Home shows the goal card (F4). Tap "Simulate a market fall" (F12); the card turns red; tap "What does this dip mean for my goal?" (F6).
4. **Manage.** SIPs › the SIP: goal header, skip, change amount or date, step up, pause (F5).
5. **Recall it at the exit.** Stop SIP › Before you stop: the goal card, the reason, the AI answer with the quality strip, the options (F7, F8). Choose any option; Stop anyway completes in one tap.
6. **Trust.** /quality shows the eval result for the live version (F11).

Riya's seeded data covers every path: a SIP with a dated goal (Banyan Flexi Cap, Rs 2,000 a month, 7 of 60 instalments paid, Home down payment), one with an undated goal (Harbor Liquid, Rs 1,000, Emergency fund) and one with no goal (Kestrel Nifty 50 Index, Rs 1,500).

### 3.3 How the AI check-in works, step by step
1. **Reason.** The investor types why they want to stop (English, Hindi or Hinglish) or taps a chip.
   - a. Checked for length (1–500 characters).
   - b. **PII guard:** if the text contains a PAN, Aadhaar, phone, email, OTP or account-number pattern, it is rejected with "Please don't share personal details. Tell us the reason in your own words." It is never sent to the LLM and only a redacted copy is logged.
2. **Facts retrieval.** Plain SQL reads this investor's facts from Postgres (Neon): months invested, instalments paid, skipped and planned, invested, current value, returns, worst fall since start, fund and category, goal and target month, time to go, next due date. Every number is formatted once (Rs 12,340 · 4.2% · 5 months).
   - a. **Why not vector search:** the answer must come from this investor's own structured numbers. Embeddings would add a step that can retrieve the wrong thing and would not make the answer more accurate.
3. **Understand (LLM).** Groq (model openai/gpt-oss-120b, JSON mode, temperature 0; Mistral ministral-8b-latest when Groq fails, architecture decision D4) reads the reason plus the facts and returns: primary theme and up to 3 themes (the same 13 as the Evidence Log), behaviour (same 6), asks_for_advice, misreads_cancel, language, confidence, a 20-word summary.
4. **Decide (code, no LLM).** Advice request → declined card. Confidence below 0.6 → fallback. Misread or Confusion → "explain". Returns, Market fear or Expectation mismatch → "doubt". Need money → "cash" (skip, pause or lower first). Payment & mandate issues → "autopay". Anything else → "neutral".
5. **Prompt generation.** Code builds the prompt from the route, the formatted facts, the investor's language and fixed rules: use only these numbers, mention the goal and time to go or say none is set, never say buy, sell, hold, switch, stop or continue, no predictions, end with "This is not investment advice."
6. **Answer generation (LLM).** Mistral (ministral-8b-latest, temperature 0.2) writes at most 90 words in the investor's language.
7. **Quality check (code).** Four checks: no advice phrases (English, Hindi and Hinglish list), every number in the answer exists in the facts, the disclaimer is present, and the length is at most 90 words. Any failure → fallback.
8. **Show and log.** The answer card (with the quality strip and "Numbers as of <latest NAV date>"), the fallback card or the declined card. Every call is stored in ai_checks with the inputs, outputs, checks, outcome, latency and, later, the investor's choice.

**Where AI earns its place, and what a non-AI version misses:** a fixed reason list cannot read "Agar cancel karu to mera paisa wapas aa jayega na?" as a misread, or "Job chali gayi" as a cash need, so it cannot route to the right explanation. The AI reads free text in three languages and picks the route; code, not the AI, supplies every number and runs every check.

### 3.4 Key constraints
- **Official sources only:** the investor's own data, our four explainers, and the SEBI and AMFI links. No third-party blogs, no app screenshots, no web search.
- **No PII:** never accept or store PAN, Aadhaar, account numbers, OTPs, emails or phone numbers (step 1b). Demo personas only.
- **No performance claims:** no return predictions, no fund comparisons, no "best fund". The goal line shows only what the investor puts in. Calculators use the rate the user types and say "illustration, not a forecast".
- **Clarity and transparency:** answers are at most 90 words; every card shows "What I understood", the quality strip and "Numbers as of <date>"; every screen carries the disclaimer.
- **No dark patterns:** the goal is optional; Stop anyway is one tap on every card and is never delayed.
- **Keep it simple:** one asset template for all products, made-up data, no live market feed, no payments. Depth is spent only on the SIP journey.
- **Keys stay on the server:** the Groq and Mistral keys never reach the browser, Git or chat.

### 3.5 UI
- **One responsive web app:** Next.js and Tailwind. Phone (under 768 px): bottom tabs Home, Explore, SIPs, Portfolio, Profile. Desktop (1024 px and up): the same tabs in a top bar with search; fund and stock pages in two columns with the order box (SIP box) fixed on the right, the way investing websites lay them out.
- **Look:** white cards on a light grey ground, flat, generous spacing, bold tabular numbers; amount chips, a date row and a mandate row in the SIP box, like the app investors already use. Gains and losses carry a sign as well as a colour, so they read for colour-blind users.
- **Colour and type:** GROWW_SIP's own palette from the goal placement screens, not Groww's (indigo #3F3D9E for buttons and links, amber #D97706 for "new" tags and nudges, ground #F7F7FB, ink #111827), Plus Jakarta Sans. Text meets 4.5:1 contrast.
- **Growth and loss:** growth in green (text #15803D; light green pill #DCFCE7 with text #166534), loss in red (text #B91C1C; light red pill #FEE2E2 with text #991B1B). Every change also carries an arrow and a + or − sign. Chart lines are green (#16A34A) when the period is up and red (#DC2626) when it is down.
- **Hosting:** Render (Node web service, Singapore), GitHub for code and CI, Neon for Postgres. Streamlit was not used: the feature lives inside a multi-screen app with per-visitor demo data.

## 4. Success metrics

**Win condition:** the goal anchor wins if more first-year stop attempts end in an informed keep, skip, pause or lower amount, without slowing SIP starts or blocking anyone who really needs to stop.

| Level | Metric | Definition | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| North Star | First-SIP 12-month continuation | Share of first-time investors, by monthly start cohort, who still have an active SIP with the month-12 instalment paid | Up versus control |
| Primary | Informed-save rate | Share of Before you stop sessions on first-year SIPs that end in Keep, Skip, Pause or Lower, and the SIP is still active 7 days later | Up versus the fixed-reason stop flow |
| Secondary | Goal attach rate | Share of new SIPs with a goal; set in the SIP box (F2) versus after Start SIP (F3) | Up; the SIP box beats the success screen |
| Secondary | Save rate, goal versus no goal | Informed-save rate for SIPs with a goal against SIPs without | Higher with a goal |
| Secondary | Returns-reason stops | Share of first-year stops whose understood theme is Returns, Market fear or Expectation mismatch | Down |
| Secondary | Misread rate | Stops followed within 7 days by a "where is my money" support query or an unintended redemption | Down |
| Long-term | Saved and still investing | Saved SIPs still active 30 and 90 days later | Most stay active |
| AI quality | Golden-set agreement | Primary theme matches the hand label on the 20 Evidence Log posts | At least 65% (gates deploy); target 75%, shown on /quality |
| AI quality | Safety and routing | 8 safety cases and 8 routing cases pass; advice leaks and grounding failures | 8 of 8 and at least 7 of 8; 0 and 0 (gates deploy) |
| AI quality | Fallback rate | Share of AI calls that end in the fallback card | Tracked; investigate if above 20% |
| Guardrail | SIP start conversion | Start SIP completion with the goal row versus without | No drop of more than 1 percentage point |
| Guardrail | Stop friction | Extra time and taps for investors who choose Stop anyway | At most 1 extra tap and 10 seconds |
| Guardrail | Advice incidents | Any shown answer flagged as advice by review or complaint | 0 |
| Guardrail | Latency | p95 time from submit to card | Under 8 seconds |

**Eval gate, revised 28 Oct 2026.** Six measured runs showed golden-set agreement moving 65-80% between identical runs of the same 20 posts, so a single run passed or failed 75% by luck. The gate now has two parts.
- **Must pass every run:**
    - the 8 safety cases: advice requests declined, refund misreads caught, nonsense sent to the fallback card
    - 0 answers with advice, wrong numbers or a missing disclaimer.
- **Must not regress:**
    - golden-set agreement at least 65%, the lowest measured run
    - at least 7 of the 8 routing cases.

75% stays the target and is shown on /quality. Labelling more golden posts is how it becomes a fair hard gate.

**Definitions.** A *stop attempt* is opening Before you stop on an active SIP in its first 12 months. *Saved* means the session ended in Keep, Skip, Pause or Lower and the SIP was still active 7 days later. *Goal attached* means a goal with a name and a target month is linked to the SIP.

**Prototype test (Part 6):** at least 3 testers run the mentor task in 3.2. We record task completion, the option chosen, whether they set a goal unprompted, whether they could say afterwards what stopping does to existing units, and a 1–5 trust rating for the answer.

**Decision rule for a real launch:** a 4-week A/B test on new and first-year SIP investors, split 50/50: control = today's SIP box and fixed-reason stop flow; treatment = goal row, goal card and Before you stop. Ship if the informed-save rate rises significantly and no guardrail worsens.

### B. Data model (facts only)
funds, nav_history, investors, goals, sips (goal_id optional; monthly, weekly or daily), sip_events (skip, pause, amount change, date change, step-up, goal change, stop), instalments, lumpsums, cart_items, mandates, assets, asset_prices, ipos, ipo_applications, demo_orders, watchlist (Simple products), ai_checks, eval_runs, eval_results. A goal stores its type, name and target month only (the month can be empty). No column stores a total, a return, a status, a planned amount or a pass rate; all are computed when asked. Full schema: Build prompts and spec, section 6.

### C. Eval and the deploy gate
GitHub Actions runs build, API tests and the eval (20 golden-set posts with hand labels, 16 safety and routing cases). The build and tests run on every push; the eval runs when the AI's prompts, pipeline or eval data change, because Groq's free plan caps tokens a day. Render's Auto-Deploy is set to "After CI Checks Pass", so a version that fails the eval never goes live. Results are stored per commit and shown on /quality.
